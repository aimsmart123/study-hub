📖 Chapter Notes, Concepts, Tips & Tricks (Class 10 Chemical Reactions & Equations) ▼
🧪 1. Chemical Reaction & Indicators Core Concept
A process in which original substances (reactants) lose their nature and identity to form new substances (products) with entirely different chemical properties.
Diagnostic Indicators of a Reaction
  • Change in State: E.g., $\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$
  • Change in Colour: Green ferrous sulphate turning reddish-brown; blue $\text{CuSO}_4$ turning light green with iron nail.
  • Evolution of Gas: Reaction of zinc with dilute acid releasing brisk effervescence of $\text{H}_2(g)$.
  • Change in Temperature: Exothermic (flask gets warm, e.g. quicklime + water) or Endothermic (flask gets cold).
Activity 1.1: Burning of Magnesium Ribbon: Clean with sandpaper (removes protective MgO layer). Burns with dazzling white flame forming white powder ash ($\text{MgO}$).
⚖️ 2. Writing & Balancing Equations Law of Mass
A chemical equation is a symbolic representation of a chemical reaction using chemical formulas of reactants and products.
Law of Conservation of Mass
Matter can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Total mass and atom counts of each element on LHS must equal RHS.
7-Step Hit-and-Trial Balancing:
1. Enclose formulas in boxes (never modify subscripts inside formulas).
2. Count atoms on LHS and RHS.
3. Balance element with maximum atoms first (usually oxygen or compound with most atoms).
4. Balance other atoms (e.g., Fe, H).
5. Check counts on both sides.
6. Write physical states: $(s), (l), (g), (aq)$.
7. Write reaction conditions (heat $\Delta$, catalyst, sunlight) above/below the arrow.
$3\text{Fe}(s) + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}(g) \to \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4(s) + 4\text{H}_2(g)$
🧱 3. Combination Reactions & Lime Cycle High Yield
A reaction where two or more reactants combine to form a single product:
$A + B \to AB$
  • Burning of coal: $\text{C}(s) + \text{O}_2(g) \to \text{CO}_2(g)$
  • Formation of water: $2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$
The Lime Cycle & Whitewashing
  • Step 1 (Slaking): Quicklime ($\text{CaO}$) vigorously reacts with water:
    $\text{CaO}(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \to \text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{Heat}$ (Vigorously exothermic, hissing sound).
  • Step 2 (Wall Carbonation): Slaked lime painted on walls slowly reacts with atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ over 2–3 days:
    $\text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{CO}_2(g) \to \text{CaCO}_3(s)\downarrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$ (Forms shiny white marble finish).
💥 4. Decomposition Reactions (3 Modes) Endothermic
A reaction where a single reactant breaks down into two or more simpler products ($AB \to A + B$). All decomposition reactions absorb energy (Endothermic).
  • 1. Thermal Decomposition (by Heat):
    • $2\text{FeSO}_4(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(s) + \text{SO}_2(g) + \text{SO}_3(g)$
    Pale green crystals turn reddish-brown; characteristic smell of burning sulphur.
    • $\text{CaCO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)$ (Limestone $\to$ Quicklime, used in cement).
    • $2\text{Pb(NO}_3)_2(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{PbO}(s) + 4\text{NO}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$
    Yellow lead monoxide residue + dense choking brown fumes of $\text{NO}_2$ gas.
  • 2. Electrolytic Decomposition (by Electricity):
    • $2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Electricity}} 2\text{H}_2(g)\text{ [Cathode]} + \text{O}_2(g)\text{ [Anode]}$
    Volume ratio $\text{H}_2 : \text{O}_2 = 2 : 1$ because each water molecule contains 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom.
  • 3. Photolytic Decomposition (by Light):
    • $2\text{AgCl}(s) \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight}} 2\text{Ag}(s) + \text{Cl}_2(g)$ (White turns grey).
    • $2\text{AgBr}(s) \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight}} 2\text{Ag}(s) + \text{Br}_2(g)$ (Used in black and white photography).
⚡ 5. Displacement Reactions Reactivity
A reaction in which a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound / aqueous salt solution:
$A + BC \to AC + B$
  • Iron nail in $\text{CuSO}_4$ (Activity 1.9):
    $\text{Fe}(s) + \text{CuSO}_4(aq) \to \text{FeSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$
    Blue $\text{CuSO}_4$ turns light pale green ($\text{FeSO}_4$); iron nail gets coated with reddish-brown copper deposit.
  • Zinc in copper sulphate: $\text{Zn}(s) + \text{CuSO}_4(aq) \to \text{ZnSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$ (Blue solution becomes colourless).
  • Lead in copper chloride: $\text{Pb}(s) + \text{CuCl}_2(aq) \to \text{PbCl}_2(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$
Reactivity Mnemonic: $\text{Zn} > \text{Fe} > \text{Pb} > \text{Cu}$ ("Zebras In Lead Cages"). Copper cannot displace iron from ferrous sulphate.
🥛 6. Double Displacement & Precipitation Ion Exchange
Reactions in which there is an exchange of ions between the reactants:
$AB + CD \to AD + CB$
Any reaction that produces an insoluble solid that separates out is called a Precipitation Reaction.
Two Crucial Board Precipitates
  • 1. Barium Sulphate (Activity 1.10):
    $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) + \text{BaCl}_2(aq) \to \text{BaSO}_4(s)\downarrow + 2\text{NaCl}(aq)$
    White precipitate of $\text{BaSO}_4$ formed instantaneously.
  • 2. Lead Iodide (Activity 1.2):
    $\text{Pb(NO}_3)_2(aq) + 2\text{KI}(aq) \to \text{PbI}_2(s)\downarrow + 2\text{KNO}_3(aq)$
    Brilliant canary-yellow precipitate of $\text{PbI}_2$ formed.
🔄 7. Redox Reactions & Agents OIL RIG
  • Oxidation: Gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen (or loss of electrons).
  • Reduction: Loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen (or gain of electrons).
  • Redox: Reactions where oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.
$\text{CuO} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Cu} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
  • Substance Oxidised: $\text{H}_2 \to \text{H}_2\text{O}$
  • Substance Reduced: $\text{CuO} \to \text{Cu}$
  • Oxidising Agent: $\text{CuO}$ (provides oxygen)
  • Reducing Agent: $\text{H}_2$ (removes oxygen)
Copper Oxidation & Regeneration (Activity 1.11):
• $2\text{Cu} + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{CuO}$ (Brown copper turns black).
• $\text{CuO} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Cu} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Black $\text{CuO}$ turns back into reddish-brown $\text{Cu}$).
🛡️ 8. Everyday Oxidation: Corrosion & Rancidity Applications
Corrosion: Deterioration of a metal due to chemical attack by atmospheric air, moisture, and acids.
  • Rusting of Iron: Forms reddish-brown hydrated ferric oxide: $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Both air ($\text{O}_2$) and moisture ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) are essential.
  • Tarnishing of Silver: Reacts with atmospheric $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ to form a black coating of Silver Sulphide ($\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$).
  • Corrosion of Copper: Reacts with moist $\text{CO}_2$ to form a green coating of Basic Copper Carbonate [$\text{CuCO}_3\cdot\text{Cu(OH)}_2$].
Rancidity & Prevention
When fats and oils are oxidised by atmospheric oxygen, they become rancid and their smell and taste change.
Prevention Methods:
1. Flushing chips bags with inert gas like Nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) to prevent oxidation.
2. Adding antioxidants (e.g. BHA, BHT).
3. Storing food in airtight containers away from light and heat.
4. Refrigeration at low temperatures.

🎯 Exam Tips & Pitfalls
  • State Symbols: CBSE marking schemes deduct $\frac{1}{2}$ mark if physical states $(s), (l), (g), (aq)$ are omitted when explicitly asked.
  • Electrolysis Ratio: Hydrogen collects at the cathode (negative) and oxygen at the anode (positive) in a $2:1$ volume ratio ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
  • Brown Gas Identification: On heating lead nitrate, the brown gas evolved is always nitrogen dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$), and the yellow residue is lead oxide ($\text{PbO}$).
  • Precipitate Colours: $\text{BaSO}_4$ is milky white; $\text{PbI}_2$ is bright yellow; copper oxide ($\text{CuO}$) is black.
